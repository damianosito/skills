# Calibration Example: Red

This is an illustrative report snapshot, not reusable market research. Recheck every competitor, price, law, and platform rule live when evaluating a real idea.

# Business Idea Feasibility Study

## 1. Idea Summary

Create a broad marketplace where professionals rent AI agents made by third-party creators. Creators receive a payout and the platform keeps a 25% commission.

> For lawyers, marketers, designers, and developers who want work completed faster, this marketplace helps them hire specialised AI agents by offering a rated catalogue of creator-built agents on demand.

## 2. Assumptions Made

- The platform hosts or executes agents, rather than only listing external links.
- Buyers can give agents files, prompts, and tool access.
- The platform pays model and infrastructure costs from its commission.
- Creators are independent sellers and may be located in multiple countries.
- The founder is a solo generalist with no existing professional-agent buyer audience or marketplace operations team.
- Legal, medical, financial, and code-producing agents are initially allowed.

## 3. Traffic-Light Verdict

**Red - Abandon / Park**

The broad concept is structurally weak despite obvious interest in AI agents. Existing platforms already own model access, creator supply, enterprise distribution, or cloud procurement, while this product would need to solve a two-sided cold start, quality verification, sandboxed execution, privacy, payouts, refunds, and professional liability at once. A 25% commission is a pricing mechanism, not a competitive advantage, and can become negative after payment fees, model/tool costs, support, and creator payouts. The fatal flaw is not buildability; it is trying to establish trust and liquidity across several high-risk professions before proving one dependable workflow.

## 5. Market and Competitor Scan

The category already includes broad agent networks, agent-building platforms, model-native stores, creator bot platforms, enterprise marketplaces, cloud procurement channels, and custom agencies. Illustrative competitor sources include [Agent.ai](https://agent.ai/), [Relevance AI](https://relevanceai.com/), [Poe](https://poe.com/), and [AWS Marketplace](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/solutions/ai-agents-and-tools). Their current products and terms must be rechecked.

Incumbents do several things well: they already have model infrastructure, user accounts, billing, creator ecosystems, enterprise relationships, or a trusted cloud procurement route. They can bundle discovery into products users already visit.

### Competitor Weakness Map

| Cluster | What it does well | Evidence to verify live | Likely weakness | Exploitable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model-native stores | Huge installed audience and simple creation | Current discovery, payout, and usage rules | Discoverability can be poor; many agents are shallow wrappers | Only with a niche trust layer, not another broad store |
| Agent-building platforms | Powerful workflows and integrations | Pricing, deployment, and template catalogues | Complex for non-technical buyers; quality varies by builder | Possibly, through a managed vertical service |
| Cloud marketplaces | Enterprise trust and procurement | Current agent listings and seller requirements | Too heavy for small buyers; setup can be intimidating | Small-business niche may exist, but distribution remains hard |
| Freelance/agency substitutes | Humans absorb ambiguity and liability | Current rates and buyer behaviour | Expensive and slow | Agents must prove outcomes, not merely be cheaper |
| General AI subscriptions | Familiar, flexible, low incremental cost | Current capabilities and plan limits | Users must design workflows themselves | A managed outcome can beat DIY for one repeated task |

Ratings are a weak trust signal because agent results vary with data, permissions, model version, and task complexity. A five-star agent can still fail on a new context. Professional verification, reproducible evals, audit logs, data deletion, limitations, and liability allocation are more important than marketplace stars.

### Missing Market Needs

`Evidence-backed needs to verify:` dependable outcomes, transparent limitations, privacy controls, permission boundaries, audit trails, and help when an agent fails.

`Speculative needs:` one catalogue for every profession, social creator profiles, and per-task agent rentals. These sound attractive but do not prove switching or payment.

### Wedge Assessment

The only credible wedge is one profession, one painful repeatable workflow, and a small set of platform-vetted agents with benchmark results and human fallback. Even that requires direct access to buyers and exclusion of regulated or high-impact actions. The broad multi-profession marketplace is not reachable by a lean founder.

### Brutal Conclusion

The apparent gap is mostly a trust problem that the proposed open marketplace would worsen. This is not a credible broad startup entry point. At best, it can be rescued as a curated vertical service with no open creator marketplace initially.

## 6. Market Demand and Potential Share

The theoretical TAM for professional AI is large but irrelevant to the entry strategy. A bottom-up first-year model is more honest: if the founder can interview 40 buyers in one niche, manually onboard five vetted workflows, and convert 10-20 organisations at GBP 100-300 per month, the rescued vertical service could test GBP 12,000-72,000 ARR. The broad marketplace has no defensible first-year SOM because neither supply liquidity nor buyer acquisition is specified.

Current classification: weak broad opportunity; potentially a small vertical managed service after radical narrowing.

## 7. Unique Selling Point Analysis

Proposed USP: `rent highly rated AI agents from independent creators and pay only for what you use`.

It is not unique. Stores, templates, bots, freelancers, and AI subscriptions already offer versions of discovery, access, and payment. A stronger proposition would be:

> Five independently tested agents for one back-office workflow, with published evals, fixed permissions, audit logs, and a human-reviewed result.

Remove the open marketplace, unrelated professions, creator self-listing, and autonomous high-impact actions.

## 8. Build Complexity and Cost

Smallest testable rescue: a landing page and manual catalogue of five vetted workflows for one niche. Execute tasks manually behind the scenes using existing models, charge for a paid pilot, and record failure modes.

Do not initially build autonomous tool access, creator dashboards, dynamic revenue sharing, public ratings, mobile apps, or multi-country tax automation.

| Cost item | Lean estimate | Type | Assumption or trigger |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Directory/concierge prototype | GBP 1,000-5,000 | One-off | Founder-led build with simple auth and payments |
| Secure hosted marketplace | GBP 75,000-300,000+ | One-off | Multi-tenant execution, sandboxing, permissions, payouts, audit logs |
| Legal, contracts, privacy, marketplace terms | GBP 5,000-25,000+ | One-off/annual | Multi-country creators and professional use raise cost |
| Insurance and security review | GBP 3,000-20,000+ | Annual/one-off | Depends on professions, data, limits, and coverage |
| Hosting, monitoring, admin | GBP 150-1,000 | Fixed monthly | Concierge stage to early hosted product |
| AI/tool execution | GBP 0.20-20+ | Per task | Highly sensitive to model, files, retries, and external tools |
| Payment and payout fees | Provider-specific | Per transaction/payout | Verify current processor and connected-account pricing |
| Support, disputes, quality review | GBP 3-30+ | Per order | Ten to sixty minutes at an assumed support rate |
| Creator and buyer acquisition | GBP 2,000-15,000 | Initial test | Events, outreach, content, demos, and onboarding |

Illustrative GBP 10 transaction: a 25% platform share is GBP 2.50. If payment/payout costs are GBP 0.80, execution GBP 1.00, expected support/refunds GBP 1.20, contribution is negative GBP 0.50 before fixed costs. At GBP 100 monthly per customer with GBP 35 variable delivery and GBP 2,000 fixed monthly cost, break-even is about 31 customers: `2,000 / (100 - 35)`.

The cost risk rises faster than revenue when users run long tasks, grant broad tool permissions, demand refunds, or need human rescue.

## 9. Monetisation

Do not use low-ticket per-agent rentals. For the rescued version, charge one niche buyer GBP 250-1,000 for a paid setup/pilot, then GBP 100-300 per month for a defined workflow allowance. Pay creators only after task economics are measured. The fastest revenue is a managed service, not commission from an empty marketplace.

## 10. Legal, Ethical, and Compliance Risks

**Legal risk: Very High.**

Risks include unauthorised professional advice, negligent outputs, insecure code, privacy and cross-border data processing, copyright and confidential information, deceptive creator claims, consumer refunds, tax and payout reporting, discrimination, unsafe external actions, and unclear liability among platform, creator, model provider, and buyer. Legal disclaimers will not cure a product that actively performs regulated or high-impact work. Qualified counsel, security review, insurance advice, data-processing agreements, creator contracts, and strict prohibited-use rules would be required.

## 11. Go-to-Market Plan

Do not market to lawyers, marketers, designers, and coders simultaneously.

First niche: one low-regulation operational role with a frequent document workflow and concentrated community.

First three channels:

1. Direct founder-led outreach to 30 operators in that exact role.
2. Partnerships with two niche consultants or software implementers.
3. Outcome-led case studies showing time saved and corrected failures.

Landing-page test: sell one managed outcome with a fixed price and clear exclusions. Path to first 10 users: manual pilots. Path to 100: only after at least 70% successful task completion without rescue, positive contribution margin, and repeat purchase.

## 12. Kill Criteria

- Fewer than 10 of 40 qualified niche buyers accept a problem interview.
- Fewer than 5 report the workflow at least weekly and value it above GBP 100 per month.
- No three paid pilots after 30 targeted proposals.
- Human rescue exceeds 20 minutes per task after the first 20 tasks.
- Contribution margin remains below 60% at realistic usage.
- Insurance, legal, or data requirements exceed the pilot budget.
- Buyers insist on regulated advice or autonomous high-impact actions to receive value.

## 13. 7-Day Validation Plan

- **Day 1:** Select one profession and one repeatable, low-risk workflow.
- **Day 2:** Recheck five direct alternatives, prices, buyer complaints, and professional rules.
- **Day 3:** Create a one-page paid managed-service offer with a sample result and exclusions.
- **Day 4:** Send 30 personalised messages to qualified buyers, not creators.
- **Day 5:** Run five interviews and manually perform two sample tasks using redacted data.
- **Day 6:** Ask for three paid pilots or refundable deposits; do not accept waitlist praise as validation.
- **Day 7:** calculate delivery time, failure rate, contribution, objections, and reissue the verdict.

## 14. MVP Recommendation

Must have: one workflow, five manually vetted task configurations, fixed inputs, fixed output schema, clear limitations, human review, payment link, and basic audit record.

Delay: creator profiles, public ratings, automated payouts, autonomous tools, APIs, mobile apps, and multi-profession browsing.

Concierge version: the founder receives the brief, runs the workflow, checks the result, and returns it manually. The core assumption is whether buyers pay repeatedly for a dependable outcome, not whether creators want to list agents.

## 15. Final Recommendation

**Abandon this idea for now because the broad marketplace combines saturated supply, an unsolved two-sided cold start, weak differentiation, fragile low-ticket economics, and very high liability. Rescue only one low-risk vertical workflow as a paid managed service, and treat a marketplace as a distant consequence of proven demand rather than the starting product.**
