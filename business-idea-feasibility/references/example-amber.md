# Calibration Example: Amber

This is an illustrative report snapshot, not reusable market research. Recheck every competitor, price, API, and legal claim live when evaluating a real idea.

# Business Idea Feasibility Study

## 1. Idea Summary

Build a mobile app for home coffee brewers to record recipes and workflows, describe how the cup tasted, and receive one practical adjustment for the next brew. Successful recipes become a personal library.

> For home pour-over brewers who get inconsistent coffee, this product helps them improve the next cup by translating taste feedback and brew variables into one evidence-based adjustment while building a personal recipe history.

## 2. Assumptions Made

- The first niche is intermediate home pour-over brewers, not espresso, cafes, or all coffee drinkers.
- The app does not claim to measure flavour objectively; it interprets user-entered taste.
- The founder can access at least 30 enthusiasts through local roasters or online communities.
- The MVP is mobile web or cross-platform, with no hardware integrations.
- AI is optional and constrained by a brew-logic rules layer.

## 3. Traffic-Light Verdict

**Amber - Proceed with Caveats**

The problem is real but not urgent: enthusiasts regularly struggle with sour, bitter, hollow, weak, or inconsistent cups, yet many already use notes, spreadsheets, roaster recipes, videos, or free calculators. Generic coffee logging is crowded and unlikely to support meaningful paid acquisition. The viable wedge is not a prettier recipe library; it is a taste-led `one next change` coach that reduces confusing multi-variable experimentation. Proceed only if targeted users repeatedly use the advice and at least a small minority pay for it.

## 5. Market and Competitor Scan

The category includes recipe/logging apps, brewing calculators, hardware-linked ecosystems, roaster guides, spreadsheets, notebooks, YouTube, and community advice. Illustrative products to recheck include [Beanconqueror](https://beanconqueror.com/), [Filtru](https://filtru.coffee/), [Acaia](https://acaia.co/), and [Barista Hustle](https://www.baristahustle.com/).

Existing tools are strong at repeatable timers, structured recipes, equipment data, and enthusiast credibility. Free content already explains extraction, ratios, grind, water, temperature, and agitation in depth.

### Competitor Weakness Map

| Cluster | What it does well | Weakness to verify | Underserved user | Wedge value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brew logs | Rich variables and history | Logging can feel like work; data does not always tell users what to change | Intermediate users with many notes but little learning | Moderate |
| Timers/calculators | Useful during brewing | Often optimise execution, not diagnosis after tasting | Users who follow recipes but dislike the result | Moderate |
| Hardware ecosystems | Accurate scale and flow data | Device lock-in and premium hardware cost | Users with ordinary scales and kettles | Real but price-sensitive |
| Education/content | Deep expertise and free access | Advice is fragmented and may suggest several changes at once | Users overwhelmed by variables | Strongest gap |
| Community advice | Human nuance and social proof | Slow, contradictory, and dependent on a well-written post | Users wanting immediate private guidance | Moderate |

The difficult product problem is causal ambiguity. Sourness can reflect under-extraction, bean character, water, roast, temperature, uneven extraction, or user vocabulary. An app that confidently prescribes one grind change without uncertainty will lose trust.

### Missing Market Needs

`Evidence-backed needs to verify:` less tedious logging, help interpreting taste, fewer variables changed at once, and advice adapted to equipment and beans.

`Speculative needs:` social recipe feeds, AI chat, gamification, automatic roaster databases, and public ratings. These may increase scope without increasing willingness to pay.

### Wedge Assessment

The lean founder can exploit the `one next change` gap because it can be delivered manually and encoded as a decision system. The wedge is reachable through roasters and enthusiast groups. It is not strongly defensible, so speed, credibility, exceptional UX, and a growing personal taste history matter more than technical novelty.

### Brutal Conclusion

There is a real product gap inside a crowded hobby market, but probably not a venture-scale opening by itself. It could become a good small paid app or an acquisition/affiliate layer for specialty coffee. A generic recipe tracker should be abandoned.

## 6. Market Demand and Potential Share

Large global coffee figures do not establish demand for this app. The relevant SAM is self-directed specialty home brewers who experiment regularly and already spend on beans and equipment. A defensible first-year SOM should be bottom-up: reach 1,000 qualified enthusiasts through three roaster/community partnerships, convert 200 to active trials, and test whether 20-60 pay. At GBP 25 annual revenue, that is only GBP 500-1,500 ARR initially, which is validation rather than a business.

A plausible strong first year for a solo niche app might be 300-1,000 paying users, but only after retention and community distribution are proven. Classification: potential lifestyle product, not currently a scalable startup thesis.

## 7. Unique Selling Point Analysis

Weak USP: `AI improves your coffee recipes`.

Stronger USP:

> Tell the app what you tasted and how you brewed; it gives you exactly one change for the next cup, explains why, and learns which adjustments work for you.

Narrow initially to one method, such as V60-style pour-over. Remove social feeds, broad coffee education, cafe tools, hardware control, and universal claims. The USP is useful but copyable; execution quality and longitudinal personal calibration must create the advantage.

## 8. Build Complexity and Cost

MVP flow: choose equipment and coffee, enter five to seven brew variables, select two or three taste descriptors, receive one recommendation with confidence and rationale, then mark whether the next cup improved.

Use a rules-first decision table reviewed by experienced brewers. AI may turn structured output into natural language but should not invent the brew diagnosis. Start as a mobile-responsive web app or lightweight cross-platform app.

| Cost item | Lean estimate | Type | Assumption or trigger |
|---|---:|---|---|
| UX, rules, and prototype | GBP 500-3,000 | One-off | Founder builds or uses a small contractor |
| Production MVP | GBP 3,000-12,000 | One-off | Auth, database, responsive UI, payments, analytics |
| Expert review and test beans | GBP 300-1,500 | One-off | Calibration sessions with brewers/roasters |
| Privacy/terms/consumer review | GBP 500-2,000 | One-off | Simple consumer app, no health claims |
| Hosting, database, email, analytics | GBP 30-150 | Fixed monthly | Early-stage managed services |
| AI text generation, if used | GBP 0.01-0.10 | Per diagnosis | Short constrained output; verify provider pricing |
| Payment processing | Provider-specific | Per transaction | Annual pricing reduces fee drag |
| Support | GBP 0.20-1.50 | Per active user/month | Mostly email and billing support |
| Beans, creator content, samples | GBP 200-1,000 | Monthly test budget | Optional growth activity, not core hosting |

At GBP 25 per year, assume GBP 22 net contribution after payment, AI, and support. With GBP 150 fixed monthly cost, operating break-even is roughly 82 annual subscribers: `(150 x 12) / 22`. If founder labour and GBP 6,000 build recovery over one year are included, break-even becomes roughly 355 subscribers: `(1,800 + 6,000) / 22`.

Cost sensitivity is manageable unless the app adds image recognition, hardware integrations, unbounded chat, a free social network, or human coffee coaching.

## 9. Monetisation

Best initial model: free five-brew trial, then GBP 20-35 per year. Avoid a high monthly subscription for a hobby utility. A one-time lifetime purchase may generate early cash but weakens funding for continued model/rule maintenance.

Fastest revenue: sell a GBP 15-25 `30-day better pour-over` concierge beta to 20 enthusiasts before building. Secondary revenue could later include ethical equipment/bean affiliates or white-labelled onboarding for roasters, but affiliate incentives must not distort advice.

## 10. Legal, Ethical, and Compliance Risks

**Legal risk: Low to Medium.**

Use normal privacy, security, subscription, refund, and consumer-protection controls. Do not make health, caffeine-safety, or medical claims. Respect copyrighted recipe text and brand assets; store user-entered recipes rather than scraping paid content. Disclose AI use if recommendations are model-generated. Avoid presenting subjective flavour advice as objective measurement.

## 11. Go-to-Market Plan

First target: English-speaking home pour-over brewers who own a grinder and brew at least three times per week.

First three channels:

1. Five independent roaster partnerships using a QR code on brew cards.
2. Helpful before/after diagnosis posts in coffee communities, following promotion rules.
3. Short search-led content around specific taste problems such as `why is my V60 hollow?`.

Landing-page test: show one interactive taste diagnosis and charge for a 30-day beta. Direct outreach: recruit 30 users who recently posted a troubleshooting question. First 10 paying users come from concierge diagnosis; first 100 should come through two roasters and repeatable problem-led content, not broad paid ads.

## 12. Kill Criteria

- Fewer than 10 of 30 qualified brewers complete three manual diagnosis cycles.
- Fewer than 60% report that at least one recommendation improved the next cup.
- Fewer than 5 of 30 pay GBP 15 for the concierge beta.
- Four-week retention among activated testers is below 25%.
- Users consistently need several simultaneous changes, making `one next change` misleading.
- Paid acquisition is needed before annual gross profit per user is known.

## 13. 7-Day Validation Plan

- **Day 1:** Restrict the offer to V60-style pour-over and define six taste outcomes.
- **Day 2:** Recheck ten tools, prices, reviews, forum complaints, and free substitutes.
- **Day 3:** Build a clickable diagnosis mock-up and a GBP 15 beta checkout.
- **Day 4:** Recruit 30 qualified brewers through roasters and permitted community outreach.
- **Day 5:** Manually diagnose each brew with a fixed decision sheet and record confidence.
- **Day 6:** Ask users to brew again, report improvement, and pay for a four-week trial.
- **Day 7:** compare completion, improvement, payment, and objections against kill criteria.

## 14. MVP Recommendation

Must have: one brew method, equipment profile, structured brew inputs, compact taste vocabulary, one recommendation, rationale, confidence, and `better/same/worse` follow-up.

Delay: social feeds, public recipes, photos, AI chat, roaster marketplace, espresso, Bluetooth scales, leaderboards, and automatic bean recognition.

Concierge version: Typeform plus spreadsheet plus manual recommendation by email. The core assumption is that one constrained next adjustment improves outcomes enough to create repeat use and payment.

## 15. Final Recommendation

**Do not build yet. Validate these assumptions first: intermediate pour-over brewers will complete repeated taste-feedback loops, one constrained adjustment improves a meaningful share of cups, and at least 15-20% of qualified testers will pay GBP 15-25 for continued guidance. If those pass, build the narrow coach, not another coffee log.**
