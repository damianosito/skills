# Calibration Example: Green

This is an illustrative report snapshot, not reusable market research. The strongest evidence in this example is user-supplied paid pilot data; competitor, price, and legal claims still require live verification.

# Business Idea Feasibility Study

## 1. Idea Summary

Build a lightweight evidence and deadline tracker for small charities that receive restricted grants. Staff forward outcome notes, attendance totals, invoices, and photos into one grant-specific evidence folder; the product reminds owners what is missing and exports a funder-ready evidence pack.

> For small charity teams who struggle to assemble grant reports from scattered evidence, this product helps them submit complete reports on time by collecting evidence against each funder's requirements throughout the grant period.

## 2. Assumptions Made

- The initial market is UK charities with 3-30 staff and several active restricted grants.
- The product is not grant discovery, accounting software, a full CRM, or an automated impact evaluator.
- Initial uploads exclude identifiable beneficiary case files; teams use aggregate outcome evidence and redacted material.
- Example founder context supplied by the hypothetical user: seven years managing charity grants, direct access to 60 charity operations leaders, and no-code product experience.
- The founder has already run 12 manual trials; eight organisations paid GBP 49 for one month and six asked to continue.

## 3. Traffic-Light Verdict

**Green - Proceed**

This narrow version solves a recurring deadline-driven problem with an identifiable buyer, existing administrative spend, and strong founder access. Most importantly, eight paid concierge trials provide better demand evidence than a waitlist or survey, and half the initial cohort has requested continued use. The product avoids competing head-on with full charity CRMs by owning one job: keeping grant-report evidence complete between award and report. Proceed with a narrow MVP, while keeping beneficiary personal data and automated impact claims out of scope.

## 5. Market and Competitor Scan

Alternatives include charity CRMs, grant-management platforms, project-management tools, shared drives, spreadsheets, email reminders, and consultants. Illustrative products to recheck include [Salesforce for Nonprofits](https://www.salesforce.org/nonprofit/), [Blackbaud](https://www.blackbaud.com/), [Submittable](https://www.submittable.com/), [Airtable](https://www.airtable.com/), and [Asana](https://asana.com/).

Established platforms are strong at broad constituent management, application workflows, finance, reporting, or flexible work management. Their breadth is also the opening: a five-person charity may not want a migration, consultant, or configurable CRM merely to stop chasing evidence before a deadline.

### Competitor Weakness Map

| Cluster | What it does well | Weakness to verify | Underserved user | Wedge value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Charity CRMs | Deep records, fundraising, integrations | Cost, implementation, training, and breadth may overwhelm small teams | Small charities without a CRM administrator | High if no migration is required |
| Grant-management suites | Application and funder workflows | Often built for grantmakers or larger portfolios, not lightweight grantee evidence capture | Small grant recipients | High for one-sided grantee workflow |
| Project tools | Flexible tasks and reminders | Users must translate each grant agreement into a system manually | Operations leads managing several grants | Moderate to high |
| Shared drives/spreadsheets | Cheap, familiar, portable | Weak completeness visibility, ownership, reminders, and consistent export | Almost all small teams | Strong substitute to beat on simplicity |
| Consultants | Expertise and accountability | Expensive and periodic; evidence may still be gathered late | Teams unable to hire ongoing support | Good concierge bridge, not software moat |

### Missing Market Needs

`Evidence-backed needs:` the 12 manual trials repeatedly needed requirement extraction, named evidence owners, missing-item visibility, deadline reminders, and a clean export. Eight paid and six continuation requests support willingness to pay.

`Speculative needs:` AI-written impact narratives, automatic outcome scoring, funder benchmarking, and a funder marketplace. These create risk and are not necessary to earn the first renewal.

### Wedge Assessment

The wedge is painful enough because reporting is mandatory, reachable because the founder already knows buyers, and feasible because the manual workflow has been repeated. It remains narrow enough for a solo founder. The key is to integrate with existing email and storage habits rather than demand CRM replacement.

### Brutal Conclusion

This is a real but bounded market opening. It can support a focused B2B SaaS or strong lifestyle business. It is not yet evidence for a large venture outcome, and adding grant discovery, CRM, or generative impact writing would dilute the advantage.

## 6. Market Demand and Potential Share

Paid pilot data is the main demand evidence. Market size should be estimated from the reachable segment, not the global nonprofit software category.

Illustrative first-year funnel: 60 warm organisations plus 300 reachable through partner networks; 120 demos or workshops; 60 trials; 30-45 paying organisations. At GBP 79 average monthly revenue, that is roughly GBP 28,440-42,660 ARR. A plausible broader beachhead could include several thousand small charities with multiple restricted grants, but official charity-register data and segment assumptions must be verified before presenting TAM.

Classification: credible focused SaaS/lifestyle business with expansion potential into accountant, consultant, and funder partnerships.

## 7. Unique Selling Point Analysis

Proposed USP:

> Turn each grant agreement into a live evidence checklist, collect proof during delivery, and export a complete reporting pack without replacing your CRM.

This is meaningfully sharper than `AI for charity grant reporting`. It targets the period after funding and before final report, when evidence is most likely to be lost. Remove application writing, donor CRM, accounting, beneficiary case management, and automatic claims about impact.

Defensibility should come from a trusted template library, integrations with existing habits, accumulated requirement structures, consultant partnerships, and high switching friction once several active grants are organised.

## 8. Build Complexity and Cost

MVP features: organisation and grant workspaces, requirement checklist, evidence owner, due date, email-forwarding or upload, missing-evidence status, reminders, activity log, and ZIP/PDF/CSV export. Use a managed relational database, object storage, transactional email, simple role-based access, and web-first responsive UI.

Do not initially build beneficiary records, AI narrative generation, funder logins, complex impact dashboards, accounting integrations, mobile apps, or a public template marketplace.

| Cost item | Lean estimate | Type | Assumption or trigger |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Product design and MVP | GBP 5,000-18,000 | One-off | Founder/no-code to small contractor build |
| Security/privacy review and contracts | GBP 2,000-6,000 | One-off | UK GDPR, DPA, retention, processor review |
| Company/accounting/insurance setup | GBP 1,000-4,000 | One-off/annual | Depends on existing entity and cover |
| Hosting, database, storage, email | GBP 100-350 | Fixed monthly | First 50 organisations with sensible limits |
| Monitoring, support, analytics | GBP 50-200 | Fixed monthly | Basic B2B operations |
| Storage/email/export compute | GBP 1-6 | Per organisation/month | Assumes aggregate/redacted evidence and limits |
| Payment processing | Provider-specific | Per transaction | Verify current UK business pricing |
| Support/onboarding | GBP 15-60 | Per organisation/month initially | 30-90 minutes during early onboarding |
| Partner workshops and outreach | GBP 300-1,500 | Monthly growth budget | Founder-led, no broad paid ads |

Base case at GBP 79 per month: payment and infrastructure GBP 6, support GBP 25, contribution GBP 48 per organisation. With GBP 600 fixed monthly costs, operating break-even is 13 customers: `600 / 48`. Including GBP 2,500 monthly founder pay raises break-even to about 65 customers: `(600 + 2,500) / 48`.

Stress case: if support reaches GBP 60 per account, contribution falls to GBP 13 and the model becomes unattractive. Standardised onboarding and a higher GBP 99-149 tier are therefore central, not optional.

## 9. Monetisation

Recommended pricing:

- GBP 59/month for up to three active grants;
- GBP 99/month for up to ten active grants and multiple staff;
- GBP 199-499 paid setup for requirement import and team onboarding;
- annual plan at roughly ten months' price after renewal is proven.

No permanent free plan. Offer a 30-day trial only after a setup call or imported grant, because empty workspaces do not demonstrate value. The fastest revenue is continued concierge-plus-software service for the six organisations already requesting renewal.

## 10. Legal, Ethical, and Compliance Risks

**Legal risk: Medium.**

The main risk is uploading personal or special-category beneficiary data. The MVP should prohibit unnecessary identifiable case data, provide redaction guidance, set retention controls, encrypt data, use role-based access, log exports, document processors, and offer a data-processing agreement. The product must not invent impact evidence or imply that an export guarantees funder acceptance. Qualified UK privacy counsel should review terms, privacy notice, DPA, retention, and breach response before wider launch.

## 11. Go-to-Market Plan

First target: UK charity operations or grants managers in organisations with 3-30 staff and at least three active restricted grants.

First three channels:

1. Convert the six continuation requests into three-month paid subscriptions.
2. Run evidence-readiness workshops with charity finance consultants and local infrastructure bodies.
3. Publish practical templates for `what evidence should we collect during a grant?` with an embedded readiness check.

Landing-page test: promise `Know what evidence is missing 30 days before the funder asks`, show the requirement-to-export flow, and offer a paid setup. Direct outreach: 30 warm introductions from the founder's network. Path to first 10: existing pilots plus referrals. Path to 100: partner-led workshops, consultant accounts, and case studies proving reduced report-preparation time.

## 12. Kill Criteria

- Fewer than 4 of the 6 continuation requests convert at GBP 59 or more per month.
- Fewer than 10 of 30 warm qualified organisations start a trial.
- Fewer than 50% of trials import a real grant and upload evidence twice within 30 days.
- Three-month logo retention is below 75% among activated customers.
- Median onboarding remains above two founder hours after 20 customers.
- Median ongoing support exceeds 45 minutes per organisation per month.
- Customers require identifiable beneficiary case management to receive value.
- No partner produces at least five qualified leads after two workshops.

## 13. 7-Day Validation Plan

- **Day 1:** Define the exact small-charity segment and renewal offer for the six continuation requests.
- **Day 2:** Recheck charity CRMs, grant platforms, prices, complaints, privacy expectations, and official charity counts.
- **Day 3:** Build a clickable requirement-checklist and evidence-export prototype using one redacted real grant.
- **Day 4:** Send paid three-month offers to six existing testers and 20 warm prospects.
- **Day 5:** Observe five users organise a live grant without coaching; record confusion and onboarding time.
- **Day 6:** Collect subscription payment, not verbal intent, and secure one partner workshop date.
- **Day 7:** compare activation, payment, support time, and privacy objections against kill criteria.

## 14. MVP Recommendation

Must have: grant workspace, requirement checklist, owner and due date, evidence upload/forwarding, missing-status view, reminders, audit log, and clean export.

Nice to have later: template library, consultant portfolio view, storage integrations, duplicate detection, and funder-specific exports.

Concierge version: continue turning grant agreements into checklists manually, onboard each team, and generate exports by hand while recording repeated steps. A no-code database and automation stack is acceptable for the first 10-20 customers if access control and data processing are adequate.

Do not build AI impact writing, beneficiary records, grant discovery, CRM, accounting, funder portal, or mobile apps. The core assumption is that ongoing evidence completeness, not final-week report writing, produces renewal-worthy value.

## 15. Final Recommendation

**Proceed with a very narrow MVP focused on requirement-linked evidence collection and export for small UK charities with multiple restricted grants. Convert the six continuation requests first, enforce strict data minimisation, and refuse adjacent features until activation, support time, and three-month retention pass the kill criteria.**
