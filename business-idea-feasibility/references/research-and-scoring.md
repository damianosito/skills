# Research and Scoring Guide

## Evidence Hierarchy

Use the strongest available source for each question:

1. Laws, regulators, official platform documentation, pricing pages, filings, and first-party product pages.
2. Credible market datasets, government statistics, peer-reviewed research, and transparent industry reports.
3. App-store reviews, G2/Capterra/Trustpilot, Reddit, forums, support threads, and public customer interviews for pain and complaints.
4. Search snippets, directories, founder claims, affiliate posts, and SEO articles only as discovery leads.

Triangulate important claims. A competitor's marketing page proves what it claims, not that customers receive the promised outcome. A complaint proves one experience, not market-wide failure.

## Search Checklist

Search combinations of:

- `[idea] app`, `[idea] software`, `[idea] marketplace`, and `[problem] solution`;
- `[competitor] pricing`, reviews, complaints, alternatives, refund, privacy, security, API, and shutdown;
- site-specific results for app stores, Chrome Web Store, Product Hunt, G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, Reddit, GitHub, and relevant professional communities;
- `[problem] spreadsheet`, consultant, agency, manual process, template, course, WhatsApp, email, and forum to expose substitutes;
- current laws, regulator guidance, platform rules, licences, insurance, tax, safeguarding, and data-location requirements;
- search trends, community frequency, job posts, paid products, and procurement evidence for demand.

Research should try to disprove the idea. Stop when additional sources are repeating the same evidence and the material uncertainties are explicit.

## Competitor Weakness Discipline

Use a matrix when useful:

| Cluster | Strength | Evidence-backed weakness | Underserved user | Switching/payment evidence | Exploitable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not claim a weakness merely because a feature is absent. Determine whether:

- users repeatedly complain;
- the missing capability blocks an important workflow;
- users pay for workarounds;
- the proposed user segment is large and reachable enough;
- the weakness results from deliberate positioning or difficult economics;
- solving it creates new support, liability, or complexity costs.

Common fake gaps include `more AI`, `better design`, `all tools in one place`, `community`, `ratings`, `cheaper`, and `for everyone`. These can become real only when tied to a painful job and credible distribution.

## Market Sizing

Prefer bottom-up sizing:

`reachable accounts x plausible annual spend x plausible penetration`

Show low, base, and high assumptions. Distinguish:

- TAM: all theoretical spend in the category;
- SAM: spend in the selected geography, segment, and use case;
- SOM: customers realistically reachable with the founder's channel, sales capacity, and first product.

Do not use a huge category TAM to hide a weak entry strategy. First-year SOM should normally be constrained by outreach volume, conversion, onboarding, churn, and support capacity.

## Cost and Break-Even Model

Use:

`net revenue per sale = price - taxes collected by seller - discounts - refunds`

`contribution per sale = net revenue - payment fees - API/compute - data - fulfilment - variable support - partner/creator payout`

`monthly break-even customers = fixed monthly costs / monthly contribution per paying customer`

State whether estimates include founder pay, VAT/sales tax, contractor labour, insurance, and customer acquisition. For freemium products, model free-user cost and paid conversion. For marketplaces, model both sides and the time/cost of quality control.

Use at least two stress cases where material:

- API or compute costs double;
- heavy users consume 5x the expected allowance;
- paid conversion is half the base case;
- support time reaches 20-30 minutes per customer per month;
- refund, fraud, moderation, or chargeback rates rise;
- sales cycles delay cash collection.

## Scoring Calibration

### Problem severity

- `1`: novelty, convenience, or entertainment with no repeated pain.
- `3`: real but tolerable pain with common substitutes.
- `5`: frequent, costly, risky, or mandatory problem with an active budget.

### Market demand

- `1`: no credible behaviour or payment evidence.
- `3`: active discussion and substitutes, but payment uncertain.
- `5`: repeated purchasing, growing budgets, and clear buyer urgency.

### Competition gap and USP

- `1`: clone with no reachable niche or switching reason.
- `3`: plausible niche distinction, weakly proven.
- `5`: evidence-backed underserved workflow with a compelling, difficult-to-copy advantage.

### Monetisation

- `1`: ads, vague commission, or low willingness to pay cannot cover delivery.
- `3`: plausible pricing but untested unit economics.
- `5`: buyer, budget, price, margin, and renewal logic are all credible.

### Build and startup cost

- `1`: capital-intensive, research-heavy, permission-dependent, or beyond the available team.
- `3`: feasible after substantial narrowing.
- `5`: concierge test in days and robust MVP in weeks at low cost.

### Go-to-market

- `1`: mass consumer acquisition, two-sided cold start, or inaccessible enterprise buyers.
- `3`: reachable niche but uncertain conversion or long sales cycle.
- `5`: founder has direct access to a concentrated buyer group and a repeatable channel.

### Legal/compliance safety

- `1`: likely prohibited, licensed, unsafe, or exposed to severe liability.
- `3`: manageable with careful scope, contracts, privacy, and review.
- `5`: ordinary low-risk product with limited sensitive data and clear compliance path.

### Defensibility

- `1`: prompt wrapper or generic feature copied immediately.
- `3`: workflow, brand, niche distribution, or proprietary data may compound.
- `5`: strong network, data, regulatory, technical, switching-cost, or distribution moat.

### Founder fit

- `1`: conflicts with skills, access, budget, or working constraints.
- `3`: learnable but no special advantage, or context is mostly unknown.
- `5`: strong domain credibility, customer access, execution ability, and sustainable motivation.

### Speed to validation

- `1`: key risk needs years, licences, network effects, or large capital.
- `3`: meaningful evidence in weeks to months.
- `5`: qualified interviews and payment test within seven days.

## Fatal-Flaw Overrides

Use Red regardless of total when a core dependency is unavailable or implausible, such as:

- platform rules block the product;
- unit economics are structurally negative;
- required data or licences cannot be obtained;
- the product creates unacceptable regulated-advice or safety exposure;
- value requires a network that cannot be seeded credibly;
- distribution cost predictably exceeds value;
- the proposed advantage disappears when compared with an incumbent's bundled feature;
- validation itself requires disproportionate capital or time.

## Validation Quality

Evidence strength, from weak to strong:

1. Likes, compliments, survey intent, and waitlist email.
2. Qualified interview plus evidence of current behaviour.
3. Time commitment, data access, introduction, or signed pilot discussion.
4. Deposit, pre-order, paid concierge trial, or paid pilot.
5. Repeat use, renewal, referral, or expansion at viable contribution margin.

Design the seven-day plan to climb this ladder, not maximise vanity metrics.
